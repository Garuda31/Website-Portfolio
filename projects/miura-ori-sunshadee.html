Miura-Ori Sunshade

The Miura-Ori Sunshade was developed during the Bucky Lab, a hands-on design course at TU Delft focused on Future Façades, in collaboration with the façade cladding company Aldowa. Working in a team of five, we started by individually proposing façade concepts to tackle overheating in glazed buildings, then combined our ideas into one shared design vision: a dynamic sunshade that could be mounted on existing façades and fold along two axes to block solar heat without losing the view outside.


After testing several folding patterns and mechanisms through a morphological study and multiple physical models, we settled on a Miura-Ori based origami pattern combined with a roller railing backstructure and a cable and pulley mechanism. The panels fully open to maximize shading during peak sun hours and retract to let in daylight when shading isn’t needed, and can be customized with different perforation patterns depending on the climate. We built and tested a 1:1 scale prototype to validate the folding behaviour, appearance and feasibility of the system.


Tools/Approach:

Rhinoceros, Grasshopper (Crane plugin), 3d printing, model making, laser cutting


Collaborators:

Purvi Sharma, Stefan Beeldsnijder, Rafif Cahyadi Agung, Yannick Huisinga
